# Standard repair visits

1. Every assigned technician accepts the visit. The primary prepares the daily kit; support views the same kit.
2. On the scheduled Philippine date, the primary goes En Route, records arrival, and selects Start Work.
3. In Inspection and quotation, get AI suggestions from the customer's reported problem, or enter the technician's findings and diagnosis. Select only diagnoses and parts confirmed on site. Suggested labor uses company rates and matched parts use catalog selling prices. Unmatched parts need a confirmed supplier price. A technician can set a custom labor amount with a recorded reason; the server computes the final total.
4. Select Submit inspection and quotation. Both admin and customer are notified. The primary technician can record the customer's verbal on-site decision against the exact submitted quotation: Repair now, Repair later, or Cancel repair. The record identifies the customer, the technician who captured the decision, the method, the quotation total, and the time. No additional admin confirmation is required for this on-site decision. Online customer decisions and staff review remain available; quotations returned for changes must be corrected and resubmitted.
5. Open the customer repair decision screen. It checks all crew members' schedules, daily kit readiness, issued tools, and parts actually received at the site. Reading the screen previews the quotation without saving an approval.
6. Repair now starts only when time, tools, and parts are ready and the customer decision is recorded. If a listed tool is unnecessary for the diagnosed repair, the primary technician can mark it **Not required** in the Repair now screen, confirm that the repair and tests can proceed without it, and record a reason. This decision applies only to the current repair visit; it preserves stock and custody records and can be changed back to Required before work starts. Reviewed Daily Preparation alternatives and not-required recommendations are also recognized. Missing repair parts and team availability checks remain required. The same-day repair appears in Admin > Appointments > Repair Scheduling for monitoring. A nearby purchase needs customer consent, a named supplier with confirmed availability, and enough time for the trip and repair. Technician purchases need an owned receipt and actual total buying cost; customer-provided parts need technician verification. Recording a purchase never starts the repair automatically or changes the accepted quotation.
7. If another visit is needed, record the reason and up to three preferred future dates. Collect the inspection and completed core-service payment first. The request appears in Admin > Appointments > Repair Scheduling. Operations confirms capacity and assigns the follow-up; fresh crew invitations preserve the earlier visit's history.
8. After starting the approved repair, record repairs and tests for each approved appliance. Continue to payment and completion proof using the existing collection and proof workflow.

Cancel repair records the customer's reason and declines only the quoted repair. Inspection fees and completed core services remain payable. Paid inspections can close; unfinished core services remain active. Cancellation does not refund money or return equipment. Repairs already started, completed, or paid require staff review for cancellation and any refund.

Company parts are issued through Daily Preparation. Nearby purchases create separate buying-cost records and pending expense review; they do not deduct warehouse stock. External parts are excluded from duplicate company-kit demand. Repair work and nearby purchases never override another job, approved leave, the saved shift, or unanswered team invitations.

The work plan applies to standard repair and mixed service bookings. Large projects retain their project work-order workflow. No migration or data reset is required.
